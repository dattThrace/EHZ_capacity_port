                                          SYMPLECTICALLY SELF-POLAR POLYTOPES OF MINIMAL CAPACITY

                                                                                    MARK BEREZOVIK

                                                 Abstract. In this paper we continue the study of symplectically self-polar convex bodies
                                                 started in [3]. We construct symplectically self-polar convex bodies of the minimal Ekeland–
                                                 Hofer–Zehnder capacity. This in turn proves that the lower bound for the Ekeland–Hofer–
                                                 Zehnder capacity for centrally symmetric convex bodies obtained in [1] cannot be improved.
                                                 We also make some numerical experiments and speculations regarding the minimal volume of
                                                 symplectically self-polar convex bodies.

                                                                                    1. Introduction
                                            Consider the standard symplectic form ω in R2n . For a convex set X ⊂ R2n define its
                                          symplectically polar convex set as
                                                                        X ω = {y ∈ R2n | ∀x ∈ X ω(x, y) ≤ 1}.
                                          Now let X be a convex body with the origin in its interior. We call such a convex body X
                                          symplectically self-polar if X = X ω . It is important to note that every such body is centrally
                                          symmetric [3, Lemma 3.1].

                 4. Symplectically self-polar bodies of minimal volume
   In this section we discuss the conjecture that the bodies P ⋉n have the minimal volume among
the symplectically self-polar convex bodies.
   Let us start with the two-dimensional case. The fact that Ekeland–Hofer–Zehnder capac-
ity coincides with the volume of a convex body in dimension 2 and Theorem 1.1 imply that
vol(X) ≥ 3 for a symplectically self-polar convex body X ⊂ R2 . Therefore, P has the minimal
volume among all two-dimensional symplectically self-polar convex bodies.
   The next theorem, motivated by the referee’s question, shows that P is the only minimizer
(up to linear symplectomorphism).
Theorem 4.1. Let X ⊂ R2 be a symplectically self-polar convex body such that vol(X) = 3.
Then X is linearly symplectomorphic to P .

Lemma 4.2. Let X ⊂ R2 be a symplectically self-polar hexagon. Then X is linearly symplec-
tomorphic to P .
Proof. Let v be some vertex of X. By Lemma 2.4 there exists a point w ∈ X such that
ω(v, w) = 1. Since X is a convex polygone, the linear function ω(v, ·) attains its maximum on
some vertex of X. Thus, we can assume that w is a vertex of X. Up to an appropriate linear
symplectomorphism we can assume that v = (1, 0) ∈ R2 and w = (0, 1) ∈ R2 . Since X is a
centrally symmetric hexagon its vertices are ±v, ±w, ±u for some u. Without loss of generality
we can assume that u = (xu , yu ) ∈ R2 for xu , yu > 0. Since X is symplectically self-polar we
have ω(v, u) ≤ 1 and ω(u, w) ≤ 1, hence xu , yu ≤ 1. Therefore X ⊆ P and P ω ⊆ X ω . Thus,
X = P , since X = X ω .                                                                      □

Remark 4.8. The upper bounds vol X ≤ π n /n! for the volume and cEHZ (X) ≤ π for the
capacity of a symplectically self-polar convex body X ⊂ R2n have been obtained in [3]. The
first inequality straightforwardly follows from the Blaschke–Santaló inequality [11]. Moreover,
if vol X = π n /n!, then X is linearly symplectomorphic to the unit Euclidean ball (this is
a consequence of the Blaschke–Santaló inequality when equality is achieved). The second
inequality is not so straightforward and the proof is based on Clark’s variational method [4] and
the two-dimensional Blaschke–Santaló inequality. It is known that the equality cEHZ (X) = π
is achieved for the unit Euclidean ball. In dimension 2 it is known that the equality is achieved
only for the images of the unit Euclidean ball under the action of linear symplectomorphisms
since cEHZ (X) = vol(X). However, in higher dimensions it is unknown whether the equality is
achieved for anything other than the unit Euclidean ball (up to linear symplectomorphisms).

                                    5. Numerical experiments
  This section explains the theoretical basis of numerical experiments, partially explains their
technical implementation, and demonstrates results. A reader interested in particular program-
ming implementation may find the code on GitHub1.
  To continue the discussion of algorithms let us make several simple statements.
Lemma 5.1. Let K ⊂ R2n be a centrally symmetric convex polytope. The inclusion K ⊆ K ω
holds if and only if for every two vertices v, w of the polytope K, ω(v, w) ≤ 1.
Proof. In one direction it follows from the definition of K ω . In the opposite direction, let
x, y ∈ K. If we consider ω(x, y) as a function of the first variable ranging over K, then it
attains a maximum on some vertex v. So ω(x, y) ≤ ω(v, y). Similarly, consider this function as
a function of the second variable. Then there is a vertex w such that ω(v, y) ≤ ω(v, w) ≤ 1. □
  1Link to the GitHub repository.

Lemma 5.2. Let K ⊂ R2n be a centrally symmetric convex polytope such that K ⊆ K ω . Let S
be a centrally symmetric subset of V (K ω ) such that ω(v, w) ≤ 1 for every v, w ∈ S. Then the
polytope M = conv(K ∪ S) satisfies M ⊆ M ω .
Proof. For a point p ∈ K ω \ K consider Rp = conv{±p} ∪ K. Then Rp ⊆ Rpω . Indeed, from the
definition of Rp it follows that every point z ∈ Rp can be written as z = tx ± (1 − t)p, where
x ∈ K and t ∈ [0, 1], therefore, for any two points z1 , z2 ∈ Rp ,
                      |ω(z1 , z2 )| = |ω(t1 x1 ± (1 − t1 )p, t2 x2 ± (1 − t2 )p)| ≤
                       ≤ t1 t2 + (1 − t1 )t2 + t1 (1 − t2 ) = t1 + t2 − t1 t2 ≤ 1.
   Consequently, it is sufficient to show that for p ∈ S, the inclusion S ⊂ Rpω holds. Let q ∈ S
and z = tx ± (1 − t)p, where x ∈ K and t ∈ [0, 1]. Since q ∈ K ω , then |ω(q, x)| ≤ 1, and since
q, p ∈ S, then |ω(q, p)| ≤ 1, and, consequently, |ω(q, z)| ≤ 1. Thus, S ⊂ Rpω .                □

An algorithm for generating symplectically self-polar polytopes. To start with, we
have a centrally symmetric convex polytope K ⊂ R2n such that K ⊆ K ω .
   (1) Construct the polytope K ω = JK ◦ .
   (2) Choose a maximal centrally symmetric vertex subset S of the polytope K ω satisfying
       assumptions of Lemma 5.2.
   (3) If it happens that S equals to the vertex set of K ω , then K ω ⊆ K by Lemma 5.1.
       Consequently, K ω = K and we halt the algorithm. Otherwise, replace the polytope K
       by the polytope conv K ∪ S and go back to the step 1.
Question 5.3. It is not clear whether this algorithm stops after finitely many steps. In practice,
it turns out that it does, at least in dimension 4. Is there a way to prove this in arbitrary
dimension? If yes, then together with Zorn’s lemma (see [3, Lemma 6.6]) this implies that the
set of symplectically self-polar polytopes is dense among all symplectically self-polar convex
bodies (in Hausdorff metric).

were carried out using fractions, without using approximate values. Only the final volume, if
necessary, was converted to an approximate decimal fraction for the convenience of interpreting
the results.

Enumeration of all symplectically self-polar −1/0/1 polytopes.
Definition 5.7. A polytope K ⊂ Rn is called −1/0/1 polytope if its vertices lie in the set
{−1, 0, 1}n .
   For the enumeration of all symplectically self-polar −1/0/1 polytopes in dimension 2n the
following algorithm was used:
    (1) Look for an inclusion-maximal set R ⊂ {−1, 0, 1}2n such that |ω(v, w)| ≤ 1 for every
        v, w ∈ R.
    (2) Construct the polytope KR = conv R. By Lemma 5.1 it turns out that KR ⊆ KRω .
    (3) Move on to the polytope KRω and check the condition |ω(v, w)| ≤ 1 for its vertices. If it
        holds, then the polytope KR is symplectically self-polar. Otherwise, the polytope KR
        is not symplectically self-polar and we just ignore the set R.
   If we go through all sets R from the bullet point 1, we will find all symplectically self-polar
−1/0/1 polytopes.
